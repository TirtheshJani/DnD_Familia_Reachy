# Nesh, the Waking Domain: Canon from the DM's Setting Document

Source: the DM's printed setting booklet (pages 4 and 5 of "The Song" chapter, the "Welcome to Nephria" handout, and the Declarations and Doctrines pages), photographed 16 September 2026. This is the DM's written canon and takes precedence over anything reconstructed from recordings in files 01 to 09. Paraphrased throughout; the booklet itself is the authority on exact wording.

## The world is called Nesh

The setting is Nesh, which the learned call the Waking Domain. Where Faerun is layered history, Ravenloft is dread, and Eberron is magic-as-technology, Nesh is defined by a handful of eccentricities set out below.

## Cosmology

Standard D&D cosmology applies around it: Inner Planes (Material Plane, Elemental Planes, Feywild, Shadowfell), Outer Planes (the Lower Planes of exploitation, the Upper Planes of virtue, where the pantheons live and where mortal souls normally go unless claimed), and Transitory Planes (Ethereal, Astral Sea, Fugue Plane). Nesh is none of these.

Nesh is a demiplane drifting in the Astral Sea: a pocket of creation formed by powerful magic, divine intervention, or extraordinary accident. It is not a "real world" in the sense most mortals mean, though almost no one living there knows that. Cut off from the normal cycle of life and death, it behaves more like an Outer Plane, an expression of a philosophy, than a living world. The themes it embodies are ambition, hunger, and rhythm, and these recur in daily life and in how the world grows.

## Where the people came from

Everyone in Nesh descends from beings who came from somewhere else: brought by magic, banished, abducted, or carried off when whole towns were torn from their home worlds and set down in Nesh. This still happens. People are pulled in from other worlds without warning, as if on a whim. Nesh has a parasitic relationship with the worlds beyond it, reaching out and taking people, places, and perhaps things it wants. To Neshians this is unremarkable: everyone knows people sometimes vanish from other worlds and turn up here.

## Death and the Song

After roughly a sennight (a week) in Nesh, an outsider becomes bound to the plane. When they die, their soul descends into the Waking Domain instead of travelling to the Outer Plane it was destined for.

Neshians call this the Song: a rhythm running through the world that calls the dead back. Life is part of a greater rhythm; when one life's rhythm ends, it begins again. Farewells are "May the Song take you into greatness" or "May the Song take you into glory." The learned understand the mechanism (souls return to the cycle of life within Nesh); ordinary people have given it a name and a philosophy.

## Gilding (canon detail)

While the soul returns to the Song, the body gilds. Decaying tissue is gradually replaced by naturally occurring metals and minerals. It usually starts in the bones and spreads outward, breaking the skin as lichen-like, crystalline, or mineral growths. The metals are pure elements, not alloys, and vary from person to person and perhaps by environment. Gold is the most recognisable and culturally weighted; silver, copper, lead, and many others occur. No one knows what decides the metal. One scholarly hypothesis ties it to burial depth and subterranean conditions, but it has never been demonstrated.

## Memory and the ocean

The ocean is a basin of memories. Its waters are amnestic: contact or drinking saps memory, narrative, and personhood. The whole water cycle is corrupted to some degree (rain, rivers, lakes, fog), and society has adapted. Cleaner water exists far to the north, but most places are tainted somewhat.

Consequences: rain wards (simple cantrips that shield a person from precipitation) are everywhere, and the ritual spell Purify Food and Drink is widely known. People know the water is dangerous even if they do not understand why.

## Hollows (canon detail)

Prolonged exposure to Styxian amnesia strips recent memories, then core memories, then the sense of self. The result is a Hollow: an invisible humanoid with no personhood, still physical, still wandering countryside and city streets, still registering to others as a presence. Hollows are not hostile, but if caught in an attack they fight back ferociously with reflective, radiant, and piercing power, while staying invisible.

## The Titans of Memory

Two primordial forces within the ocean are emanations of these effects.

- Eris, the Seven-Headed Queen, the Consuming Tide: consumer of the memories the sea takes; the sea's wrath, possessiveness, jealousy, and devouring nature.
- Thatos, the Memory Tide: preserver of memories taken or lost, gathering and protecting what the water strips from others. Believed able to grant immunity to the sea's amnestic effects, though how and how far is poorly understood.

## Arriving at Nephria (the "Welcome to Nephria" handout)

Travellers follow the Blackwater for about a week as it widens toward the sea, smelling the city before seeing it: salt, flowers, smoke, something stranger. The city resolves out of haze as black walls, then towers. A long arching bridge of dark glass and stone runs from where the Blackwater meets the coast; obsidian rails carry silent monorail cars across it. Suspended gardens hang above the walls, so pollen mixes with sea air. Pressed against the walls is a crowded gate settlement.

Northreach Crossing (canonical spelling; files 01 to 09 say North Reach Plaza) is that settlement: ramshackle streets of tents, wagons, cookfires, shrines, and stalls; pilgrims, merchants, labourers, and opportunists; unfamiliar languages; nightly fireworks beyond the walls. People wait days, weeks, months for work permits, family sponsors, or approval. The crossing is a sieve, filling faster than the city can absorb.

The Meridian's monorails hum overhead at night, their chimes carrying over the camp, and stories circulate of illusions that talk like living people, voices carried across counties through black glass, endless nightlife, fortunes made in weeks, and people swallowed by the city and never seen again. Eventually your name is called; you stand before the Gatewarden and account for yourself (family inside, work waiting, a place to stay); papers change hands; hours pass; the gates open.

## Government of the Realm

### The Twelve Declarations
The Sovereign's personal proclamations, spoken at the Realm's founding and preserved unaltered by the Gold Masks. Every act of government derives legitimacy from them; nothing lawful may contradict them. In paraphrase:

1. Sovereignty: the Realm belongs to the Sovereign and is governed through her.
2. Deference: no public honour may exceed that owed to her.
3. Dominion: the Realm's lands are held in her name by faithful stewards.
4. Covenant: everyone living under her peace owes duty in return.
5. Divine Right: divinity belongs first to her.
6. Civilization: wherever civilisation advances, her will advances with it.
7. Beauty: creation without beauty dishonours the Realm.
8. Self: no one shall be made to live against their nature.
9. Revelry: the fruits of labour are meant to be enjoyed.
10. Hospitality: no guest under her protection shall be denied bread, roof, or judgment.
11. Blood: her blood binds its bearer to the Realm and lends her authority to that bearer.
12. Intercession: no matter in the Realm is beyond her hand if she chooses to extend it.

### The Twenty-Five Doctrines
Authoritative interpretations of the Declarations, compiled and maintained by the Gold Masks over three centuries. Provinces, chartered cities, covens, and other recognised authorities may make their own laws, but all must be consistent with Declarations and Doctrines. Auditors, inquisitors, and magistrates measure every office and decree against them. In paraphrase:

1. Of Gold: the Gold Masks are the Sovereign's voice, hand, and judgment in ordinary affairs, interpreting where she has not spoken.
2. Of Stewardship: office is stewardship, not possession; authority is lent for a season and returns when trust is withdrawn.
3. Of Jurisdiction: stewards stay within their charge, neither usurping nor neglecting, unless higher authority lawfully intervenes.
4. Of Interpretation: where Declarations admit many readings, the Gold Masks reconcile them in the Realm's spirit until the Sovereign says otherwise.
5. Of Silence: the Sovereign's silence neither grants nor refuses; no one may infer her will or claim authority from what she has not said.
6. Of Petition: every subject may seek judgment from their steward and, failing remedy, petition higher authority; no petition is forbidden, none is guaranteed its wish.
7. Of Citizenship: whoever lives under the Realm's peace, accepts its protection, and fulfils its duties is recognised, owes allegiance, and is protected.
8. Of Kin: those of the Sovereign's blood are the Blooded Kin, with rights and duties as she or the Gold Masks bestow.
9. Of Conduct: no law may force a soul against its true nature unless that nature violates the Declarations or the peace; virtue lies in honest expression tempered by duty.
10. Of Assimilation: those seeking the Realm's peace are taught its customs, bound by its laws, and welcomed; no willing soul stays outside the Covenant forever.
11. Of Land: every holding is a trust; no land without obligation, none neglected without consequence.
12. Of Charters: by charter the Sovereign grants privilege, burden, office, liberty, or charge beyond the ordinary; charters endure by their terms until amended, revoked, or fulfilled.
13. Of Iron: the Iron Bank of Maliburn holds the treasury, the Crowns, and the public accounts in perpetual stewardship; no steward may diminish the treasury without lawful authority.
14. Of Crowns: the Crowns (the coinage) bear the Sovereign's likeness, authority, and promise; counterfeiting, defacing, or profaning them diminishes the Crown itself.
15. Of Settlement: roads, walls, wells, halls, and markets are the shared inheritance of all who live in a settlement, not one steward's burden.
16. Of Delegation: every office and jurisdiction is exercised at the Sovereign's leave; none is inherent or beyond amendment, revocation, or recall.
17. Of Authority: the Blooded stand above lesser stewards; contested jurisdiction is judged first by the Blooded unless reserved to the Gold Masks or the Sovereign.
18. Of Precedence: when lawful authorities clash, each office yields to its superior by the order of the Realm.
19. Of Audience: no Blooded or Gold Mask may be denied the audience due their station in any office, court, hall, treasury, or seat of governance, unless the Sovereign decrees otherwise.
20. Of Loyalty: no office, charter, covenant, or steward may set itself against the Crown; every breach is answered by law.
21. Of Idolatry: no public shrine, rite, or devotion may give divine honour to any power above, equal to, or rivalling the Sovereign within the Realm.
22. Of Recognition: lawful dignity anywhere in the world exists only by the Sovereign's recognition.
23. Of Embassies: powers she recognises receive embassies that may speak within the Realm without authority over it, enjoying its peace beneath her dominion.
24. Of War: only lawful authority may make war in her name; the burden and judgment of war belong to the Crown.
25. Of Incorporation: lands and peoples brought under her peace are taught its customs, protected by its laws, and bound within its Covenant.

### What this explains about play so far
- "In the name of the Sovereign" draws on Declarations 1, 6, and 12 and Doctrines 16 and 20, but Doctrine 5 (Of Silence) means no one may claim her authority for an act she has not sanctioned, and Doctrine 24 forbids private war in her name. Clover's licence to act is real and bounded.
- The Priory's role in Nephria is a local expression of the Gold Masks' interpretive function; whether Nephria's Priory is itself staffed by Gold Masks is not stated.
- The bans on statues of anyone but the Sovereign and on certain music follow from Declarations 2 and 5 and Doctrine 21.
- Covens are named explicitly as recognised authorities that may make their own laws, which is the legal footing of the Estates.
- Declarations 8 and 9 and Doctrine 9 give the Silk Quarter's nightlife and the city's "every night is a party" posture a constitutional basis.
- The coinage is the Crown, issued through the Iron Bank of Maliburn (a place not otherwise described yet).
