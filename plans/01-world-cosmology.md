# World Cosmology and Laws

## The 30-hour day

Nephria's day is thirty hours long. The night has structure:

- Umbrella hours: the three hours before midnight. Illusion magic is stronger. (The transcript renders this as "allusion"; the DM meant illusion.)
- Deep night: begins at midnight, every night. Its length is random, from a single second to about four hours; the usual range is one minute to three hours. The longest ever recorded lasted roughly three days, about two decades ago, and nothing has come close since. A strange, unreplicated four-minute event also occurred around twenty years ago.
- Witching hours: the three hours after deep night ends. Enchantment magic is stronger.

## Deep night

At midnight the environment freezes. Rain hangs in the air, wind stops, water stills. People and creatures can still move. During deep night, eldritch horrors descend from the sky: unknowable things, orbs that jolt the mind, entities no one has catalogued. They also come down during rain events.

The world's answer is to hunker down. Go inside, make no sound, wait. Because of this, civilisation clusters in large defensible cities with only sparse, small settlements between them.

Nephria is one of the few places that answers deep night differently. It has guns. The city shoots fireworks and artillery at the horrors and treats the defence as a nightly show. See 03-nephria-city.md on the Obsidian Rail and the ADA.

## Styx water and memory

The ocean south of Nephria is the River Styx, the memory-stealing river of the lower planes, surfacing here as an entire water cycle. Ocean, fog, rain: anything that holds water drawn from that ocean carries the property of taking memories.

- Limited exposure (caught in the rain) inflicts a temporary minus 1d4 to all checks and saves for a while.
- People carry charms, perform rituals, and use magic to keep their memories in their heads. Even so, a bad memory is common. Everyone gets caught in the rain eventually.
- Perpetual exposure (the homeless, the lost) erodes the sense of self entirely. Such a person goes Hollow.

## The Hollows

A Hollow still has a physical body but has forgotten who it is. Because it has no conception of self, it is invisible. Hollows wander the streets, passive and non-hostile. You may be walking alone at night and bump into someone who is not there. Attack one and it may fight back. There is always the possibility that something is with you.

The Graywater is described as "regurgitated river water." Do not drink from it.

## Gilding

When bodies die, in addition to ordinary decomposition, metal grows from the bones and across the face. An exhumed skeleton is often solid gold or silver, or another metal. This is the gilding.

During deep night, gilding can happen at a supernaturally rapid rate; the party watched a captain gild before their eyes.

Gilded dead do not always stay down. Some rise again, awake and made of metal, with no memory of who they were. The Durnagall Estate exists partly to keep the old graves sealed.

## Reincarnation and the song

Nephrians believe that when you die your soul returns to the song, the beating heart at the centre of the world, and that in time you are sung anew and return. Some also speak of "the light" (the phrase in the transcript is unclear).

## The Sovereign's laws

The Sovereign rules the province from Xeria and issues unusual, sweeping prohibitions: no statues of anyone but her, certain kinds of music forbidden, and rules concerning amnesia. The Priory in Nephria interprets her law and finds the loopholes that let the city function. Violence "in the name of the Sovereign" is treated by some as licensed; whether that is true or a convenient story is a matter of in-fiction debate.

## Time is unstable

Temporal disturbances are part of the world. The party has already encountered a time loop on the Graywater Crossing (see 07-session-recaps.md), a hag-like woman who moved travellers through time, and the rule that entering certain timeshifted places requires passing under an arch.
